**Sales tell you what was sold; profit needs dependable cost information as well.** A busy day at the counter is encouraging, but it does not by itself establish how much the business earned after costs and expenses.

<p lang="hi">Bikri aur munafa ek hi cheez nahi hain. Daam pata ho par asli kharid cost na pata ho, to software ko profit ka andaza bana kar nahi dikhana chahiye.</p>

## Begin with a complete item example

Take a deliberately simple illustration: one item is sold for ₹100, and its genuine recorded cost is ₹70. Before other applicable adjustments, the difference is ₹30. The result depends on the cost being real and on comparing amounts consistently.

That small example is not a substitute for the application's accounting, tax or cost-allocation logic. Returns, discounts, taxes and stock-cost methods can affect the actual report. Use the reports generated from the recorded transactions rather than recreating a different calculation on the side.

## Keep unknown cost different from zero cost

Now imagine a second item sells for ₹80 but no genuine cost has been recorded. The sale is known. The profit on that item is not established by the selling price alone.

Treating the missing cost as ₹0 would make that item's apparent profit ₹80, which is not supported by the available record. The useful response is to keep the result unknown or mark the report as partial until the underlying cost data is complete.

This matters when starting a shop quickly. [Billing without full setup](/blog/kirana-billing-without-inventory-setup/) should be possible, but fast billing must not create fictional opening stock or fictional costs.

## Repair the underlying record

Use genuine purchase records, correct opening information and supported cost workflows. Check whether the item and unit in the purchase match the item and unit sold. A cost recorded for a box can be very different from a cost per piece if the pack configuration is wrong.

Do not rewrite an old transaction casually just to make a current profit card look complete. Preserve the accounting trail and use the supported correction or review workflow. An honest partial figure is more useful than a confident-looking figure built on guesses.

## Separate item margin from operating expenses

The selling-price-minus-item-cost difference is not automatically the shop's final business profit. Relevant operating expenses also matter. Record genuine expenses under the appropriate category and payment method.

Avoid double-counting supplier payments as a second expense after the stock purchase has already been recorded through its proper workflow. Similarly, collecting old customer dues is not a new sale. Confusing either distinction can make business reports difficult to interpret.

## Review what the report can actually support

When reading a profit report, ask which transactions have genuine cost data, whether the period includes returns or adjustments and whether the relevant expenses have been recorded. Follow up on missing data before making a pricing or purchasing decision from an incomplete number.
