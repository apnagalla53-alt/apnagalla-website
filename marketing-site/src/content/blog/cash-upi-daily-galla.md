**Today's sales, money received today and physical cash in the drawer are three different figures.** Daily Galla becomes useful when you can explain how recorded cash movements lead to the amount you count at closing.

<p lang="hi">“Aaj ₹2,000 ki bikri hui, par galle mein ₹2,000 nahi hain” zaroori nahi ki galti ho. Kuch payment UPI se aayi ho sakti hai, kuch udhaar ho, aur kuch cash kharch hua ho.</p>

## Split sales by the real payment method

Imagine a shop records ₹800 of cash sales, ₹600 of UPI sales and ₹300 of new credit sales. Total sales are ₹1,700. Only the ₹800 cash portion directly adds physical money to the drawer at that point.

The ₹600 UPI amount belongs in the digital-payment record. The ₹300 credit amount belongs in the customer's outstanding khata until it is paid. Calling all ₹1,700 “cash received” would hide these distinctions.

## Add collections without adding sales again

Suppose a customer pays ₹200 in cash toward last week's bill. Cash increases by ₹200 and the old customer due decreases by ₹200. Today's sales do not increase by another ₹200.

When the same collection arrives through UPI, the cash drawer does not change. Record the payment method actually used and reconcile it with the relevant transaction history. A screenshot or promise to send money should not replace checking the receipt in the shop's own bank or payment app.

## Record cash leaving the drawer

Cash expenses and cash supplier payments reduce the money present. A credit purchase, on the other hand, can increase stock and supplier dues without immediately reducing cash. A later cash settlement is the cash movement.

Keep those workflows separate so the same payment does not appear as both a supplier settlement and a new general expense. Also record supported owner or bank cash movements when they occur; money removed from the drawer does not disappear from the explanation simply because it was not a shop sale.

## Work through the closing count

Using the sales split above, take this illustrative cash record:

| Movement | Amount |
| --- | ---: |
| Opening physical cash | ₹1,000 |
| Cash sales | +₹800 |
| Old dues collected in cash | +₹200 |
| Cash expense | −₹100 |
| Supplier paid in cash | −₹300 |
| Expected physical cash | **₹1,600** |

The UPI sales of ₹600 and new credit sales of ₹300 remain outside that drawer calculation. They still matter to the business, but they are not banknotes in the counter.

Count the drawer independently. If it contains ₹1,550, the difference is ₹50. Check missing entries, wrong payment methods, change and cash removed before making a correction. A made-up sale would distort the records instead of resolving the reason.

## Make a repeatable closing habit

Use [Daily Galla](/features/daily-galla/) to review the recorded cash movements, then compare them with the physical count. Review [customer dues](/features/khata/) separately and keep UPI checks separate from cash checks.

The aim is an explainable day, not just a neat-looking total. A short [daily hisaab routine](/blog/dukaan-ka-hisab-kaise-rakhe/) makes differences easier to investigate while the transactions are still fresh in memory.
